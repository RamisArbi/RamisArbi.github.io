## Abstract

This doctoral thesis investigates the synthesis, assembly, characterization, and application of nanostructured materials fabricated through reverse diblock copolymer micelle templating. The work focuses on the controlled growth of designer nanoparticles and nanostructured surfaces for applications in photonics, optoelectronics, sensing, catalysis, and advanced functional materials.

Through the use of self-assembled block copolymer nanoreactors, strategies were developed to achieve controlled nanoparticle morphology, composition, and spatial organization. The resulting materials were evaluated using a wide range of structural, optical, and electronic characterization techniques to establish relationships between synthesis conditions, nanoscale architecture, and functional performance.

The research demonstrates the versatility of reverse diblock copolymer micellar templating as a platform for producing highly ordered nanostructures and provides insight into the design of next-generation materials for optical, electronic, and energy-related applications.
