## Course Description

This course provides an in-depth introduction to the physical principles and operational characteristics of semiconductor devices.

Students develop a foundation in semiconductor physics through the study of band theory, charge carrier transport, p-n junctions, diodes, bipolar junction transistors, photodiodes, LEDs, solar cells, and field-effect devices. The course serves as a foundation for advanced semiconductor and microelectronic device courses.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of the course, students should be able to:

- Explain semiconductor device operation using band theory.
- Analyze equilibrium and non-equilibrium carrier behavior.
- Model and evaluate p-n junction devices.
- Understand operation of photodiodes and solar cells.
- Analyze LED device characteristics.
- Evaluate bipolar junction transistor operation.
- Understand JFET device principles.
- Apply theoretical concepts to practical semiconductor technologies.

## Major Topics

- Semiconductor band theory
- Charge carriers and transport
- Non-equilibrium carrier dynamics
- p-n junctions
- Junction diodes
- Photodiodes
- Solar cells
- Light-emitting diodes (LEDs)
- Bipolar junction transistors (BJT)
- Junction field-effect transistors (JFET)

## Instructional Components

- Three weekly lectures
- Weekly tutorial
- Four laboratory experiments
- Four assignments
- Student technical presentations


## Laboratory Experience

Laboratory activities expose students to practical semiconductor characterization and experimentation.

Students complete:

- Four laboratory experiments
- Individual laboratory reports
- Device analysis exercises

Students work in pairs to prepare and deliver classroom presentations explaining assigned problem solutions using professional presentation tools.
